# UI Design

Clarity - The first Menu scene is very easy to navigate for the player. There is only one ‘Play’ button to press and the game starts. The health is also displayed by a red layer over the entire screen, getting more opaque as your health lessens and vice versa.

Learnability - The game’s item inventory bar consists of a labeled number system, where each slot corresponds to each item that can be easily switched to from the keyboard. The panting/breathing noises signal the stamina to the player/cooldown period.

Low Contrast - Our game’s first level is mostly built around the idea of a dark and scary environment, except for the middle part of the building, where the lit-up courtyard is.

Consistency - The game’s look and feel stay consistent throughout the game. The player’s inventory is always the same and at the same place, the only thing that changes is an added picture of the item you equipped.

Providing Feedback - The game provides a great number of feedback, where each held item has its own sound, dialogue, objective, and action giving the player feedback of their actions.

Minimizing Error Possibilities - When you start the game, we put barriers so that the player does not have anywhere else to go but the right room. We also use text tips/hints from the start of the game and after every event/encounter.

Providing Feedback - One of the biggest feedbacks a player can have is hearing different sounds in different environments and during different actions. We have a sound for walking through the vent, getting out of it, walking regularly, running, and more.


# Sound Design

(Silent Hill PS1)
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dqCX_LL7_88 
Walking sounds are taking place outside on asphalt and grass. By changing sounds for different materials, the game is more immersive and doesn’t get boring to listen to since you are walking a lot of the time. The sound is not overpowering for the visuals. You are walking and depending on the surface it changes noise. The sounds are not too repetitive. I think altering the pitch and volume of each step everytime it is played can help randomize the sound and avoid repetition. They probably recorded footsteps and different things crunching together to make the sound. The only other sound is ambience and it is mixed in well with differing reverbs.

A Generator/motor sound is used when you activate the electricity of the school. The low hum and loudness of it makes an oppressive atmosphere. It is very overpowering compared to the visuals since not too much is going on but the sound conveys the dreadful feeling. This corresponds to the dread and overall ambience of the town. This sound goes on for a while, I think they stretched and pitched down many things and added a lot of distortion to get this ambience. The noise is so loud that it almost hurts and is discomforting to listen too, drowning out the other noises.

The high pitched radio static that plays when an enemy is close or approaching is an interesting way to tell the player that an enemy is approaching. It is alarming to the player because of how high pitched it is, it doesn’t sound like normal radio static. The first time you hear it, it is unexpected and makes you wonder what it is. This radio is a common motif in the series and returns in each title. It is not explained how it works and can be turned off by the user, though it is usually kept on. It sounds like grinding metal screeching. It is loud and disrupts the player’s state of focus after hearing very quiet sounds for a while.

Loud metal clanking noises are played in different areas where it makes the user anxious about where the sounds come from and if there is something else in the building with them. It also is to symbolize the main monster, Pyramid head’s presence since he has a large metal head and sword. I think these were recorded in long sessions or using randomized sample players to create long stretches of seemingly random sounds coming from different areas.

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zZNfd04GO-U 
Panting and breathing noises are used a lot in Outlast and put you into the shoes of the character as you can hear his reaction to different actions and things that scare him. The actions are well timed with his grunts that sell the motion and awkwardness of a person who is not athletic, scaling the different obstacles or falling. It sounds like they actually recorded a person breathing and doing different activities to get accurate timing for the sounds. As well as to get different variations so it isn’t reusing the same sounds all the time.

Sounds are played when viewing notes. This adds  more visual cohesion to switching between normal view and reading a note. It makes reading notes more interesting and engaging. As well as more satisfying. This was probably made by ruffling pages of notebook paper in front of a microphone and editing them to sound pleasing. This probably also adds a level of calmness to moments in the game where you don’t have to be so on edge all the time but that brings your guard down.
